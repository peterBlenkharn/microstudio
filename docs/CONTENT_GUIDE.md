# Content & Writing Guide — Micro Studio

## Voice & Tone

### Brand Voice

Micro Studio's voice is **confident, approachable, and energetic**. We're a university programme that produces real, shippable games — that's genuinely impressive, and the site should reflect that pride without being boastful.

| We are... | We are NOT... |
|-----------|---------------|
| Enthusiastic | Hyperbolic or salesy |
| Professional | Corporate or stiff |
| Inclusive | Exclusive or elitist |
| Direct | Vague or wishy-washy |
| Proud of our students | Self-congratulatory |

### Tone Spectrum

Adjust tone based on context:

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Hero / Headlines | Bold, punchy | "A micro studio for BIG ideas." |
| Game descriptions | Atmospheric, evocative | "The butcher wants your flesh." |
| About / Explainer | Clear, informative | "An intensive, collaborative creative project..." |
| CTAs | Direct, inviting | "Follow the journey" |
| Student bios | Personal, authentic | Written in their own voice |
| Footer / Legal | Neutral, factual | "© University of Greenwich Micro Studio" |

## Writing Standards

### General Rules

1. **British English** — colour, programme, specialise, organise
2. **Sentence case** for headings — "Meet the teams" not "Meet The Teams"
   - Exception: game titles use their official capitalisation
3. **Oxford comma** — "mentors, industry partners, and each other"
4. **Active voice** — "Students build games" not "Games are built by students"
5. **Present tense** where possible — "Students create" not "Students will create"
6. **Short sentences** — aim for 15–20 words max per sentence
7. **No jargon** — avoid "synergy", "leverage", "deliverables"

### Numbers & Time

- Spell out one to nine; use digits for 10+
- "12 weeks" not "twelve weeks" (for emphasis, digits hit harder)
- Use an en-dash for ranges: "June–August"

### Programme Name

- Full: "Micro Studio" (two words, both capitalised)
- Never: "MicroStudio", "micro studio" (in running text), "the Micro Studio programme"
- In context: "the Micro Studio programme" or just "Micro Studio"

### University Name

- Full: "University of Greenwich"
- Never: "Greenwich University", "UoG", "Greenwich uni"

### Degree Name

- Full: "BSc Games Development"
- Never: "BA Games Dev", "Games Design", "Game Development" (singular)

## Section-by-Section Content Notes

### Hero

The hero is the first thing anyone sees. It needs to:
1. Communicate what this is (students making games)
2. Convey the energy and ambition
3. Anchor the "micro/BIG" contrast that defines the brand

**Current copy:**
> A micro studio for BIG ideas.
> Students at the University of Greenwich creating entire games in just 12 weeks.

This is strong. The "micro/BIG" contrast works well visually and conceptually. The subtitle is factual and compelling.

**Suggestions:**
- Consider adding a seasonal element: "Summer 2025" or the current cohort year
- The word "entire" could be replaced with "original" to emphasise creativity over scope

### About Panels

Three panels answering the obvious questions a visitor has:

| Panel | Current | Notes |
|-------|---------|-------|
| What It Is | "An intensive, collaborative creative project for our undergraduates—launching original student games in 12 weeks." | Good. Clear and concise. |
| What It's For | "Building real-world skills, getting published on Steam, and pushing creative boundaries in a high-energy crash course." | "High-energy crash course" is good but "crash course" undersells the depth. Consider "intensive programme". |
| Who It's For | "BSc Games Development students teaming up with mentors, industry partners, and each other to ship amazing games." | "Ship amazing games" is slightly generic. Consider specifics: "...to ship games on Steam." |

### Game Taglines

Each game card needs a short, evocative tagline (one sentence, <10 words):

| Game | Current | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| The Lost Museum | "Can you unravel the mystery of the museum?" | Good — question format creates intrigue |
| Ghrystlyst | "The butcher wants your flesh." | Punchy and atmospheric |
| Parapet | "Defend your keep from waves of skellies." | "Skellies" is charming and informal — fits the indie vibe |

### Student Bios

Bios should feel personal and authentic. They're written by the students themselves. When reviewing/editing:

1. **Third person** is preferred for consistency ("Gabriele is..." not "I am...")
2. Keep to 2–3 sentences
3. Mention: what they do/specialise in, what drives them, one personal detail
4. Fix grammar/spelling but preserve their voice
5. Avoid starting every bio with "[Name] is a..."

**Inconsistencies to fix:**
- Some bios use first person ("I am a game developer...") — convert to third person
- Some are very long (Elie's is 4+ sentences) — consider trimming
- Patryk's bio switches from third to first person mid-way

## Imagery Guidelines

- **Team photos** should feel candid and collaborative, not posed corporate headshots
- **Game art** should be the strongest visual asset the team has — key art, not screenshots
- **Profile photos** are fine as casual selfies/headshots — authenticity > polish
- **Avoid stock photos** — everything should be from the actual programme
